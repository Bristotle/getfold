The problem

Church software built abroad assumes card giving, a single independent congregation, and a connection that never drops. It has no idea what a circuit is, a society, a catechumen, or a quarterly return to a superintendent. Churches buy it, use a tenth of it, and keep the paper register anyway.

What is in the product

  • Digital Giving. MTN MoMo, Telecel Cash and AirtelTigo alongside cash, settling into the church's own account.
  • Financial Management. Tithes, offerings, funds and the figures your circuit asks for, in cedis.
  • Member Management. One register for the whole church, searchable from a phone, in your denomination's words.
  • Leadership Management. Pastor, secretary, treasurer and class leaders each with a login and a role.
  • Branch Management. Headquarters over regions over districts, each keeping its own register, figures rolling up.
  • Groups and Cells. Bible classes, fellowships, cells and societies, each with its leader and members.
  • Communication. Birthday, welcome and thank you texts, sent in the church's own name rather than ours.
  • Attendance, returns and insights. A service marked in one save, the return printed or saved as PDF, and quietly dropping attendance surfaced early.

Why it is different

The money Giving is paid directly into the church's own settlement account. It never passes through Fold's balance, and we take nothing from what members give.
The structure Six denominational structures modelled against each church's own published material: Methodist, Presbyterian, Pentecostal, Anglican, AME Zion and Baptist. Branches to any depth, with oversight from above that is read only.
The security One church cannot see another's records, enforced by the database rather than hidden behind a menu. Giving is visible only to leadership. Every change is logged, and nobody can edit the log.
